## Asymptotic strength and domain

The logarithm module proves analytic implications at infinity. The bounded-log-difference lemma allows an arbitrary filter and explicitly assumes eventual positivity of both functions. The general little-o implication uses Lean's total logarithm, so interpreting it as a power exponent still requires a positive observable. The real-power examples are eventually positive; finitely many small arguments are immaterial. No percolation exponent value or parameter-zero theorem is exported.

For the logarithmic corrections, comparison would bound bL+c log(L), with L=log(log(x)) tending to infinity. Dividing by L first forces b=0, then boundedness forces c=0. The second counterexample uses sin(x), whereas the article's earlier example uses sin(log(x)); both refute an amplitude conclusion, but they are distinct formulas. The source's anonymous witnesses also include 2x cubed and x^alpha log(x).

## Analytic fixed points versus a process

The generating-function file treats a probability mass function on the natural numbers, not a random process. Summable masses give continuity on [0,1], including at one without a moment assumption. The formal nonnegativity lemma outside that interval is not a convergence result. Monotone iteration supplies a least fixed point independently of a generation process.

Finite mean is used only where real mean summation is needed. In the supercritical argument, a finite partial mean exceeding one produces a polynomial g with g(1)>1. The geometric-sum identity then makes phi(s)<s below one, even for infinite mean. At mean at most one, excluding the point mass at one forces positive mass at zero, which supplies strictness in the strengthened Bernoulli inequality. The point mass at one gives the identity function and least fixed point zero. The Bin(2,3/4) witness has mean 3/2 and least fixed point 1/9.
