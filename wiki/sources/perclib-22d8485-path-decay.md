## Probability enters before enlargement

Only simple paths receive the distinct-coordinate cylinder interpretation. Their continuation lists injectively encode both path and endpoint. Enlarging to all lists is then a deterministic nonnegative-sum comparison. For the two-vertex half-open bond, going out and back has deterministic weight one quarter, whereas requiring that single bond open has probability one half. The source includes this witness explicitly. Zero off-diagonal profiles, arbitrary loops, singleton and empty vertex types are also covered.

For support-distance tails, paths shorter than the prescribed lower bound have a zero-probability edge. The summed tail counts each path at one endpoint, rather than summing the same unrestricted path count for every endpoint. Disconnected vertices satisfy the all-walk lower-bound premise for every k and have zero connection probability. The source's natural-valued graph distance is zero on disconnected pairs; the article uses the all-walk formulation so this convention cannot be mistaken for an extended distance. The two-vertex quarter-probability and zero-profile examples witness the source statements. The support graph may be complete in a long-range model, so these bounds need not yield Euclidean decay.

## Degree bounds and endpoints

The finite list cover excludes the preceding vertex at each step; it may contain walks that fail full self-avoidance, which only enlarges the cover. A fixed-length simple-path event is a finite union of cylinders, so its probability estimate requires neither ambient countability nor off-host restrictions. Countability enters almost-sure host containment and the full-cluster conclusions. Local finiteness makes bounded walk balls finite and allows an infinite cluster to supply paths of every finite length; an infinite star would invalidate this implication without local finiteness.

The two-point series excludes equal endpoints, and begins at positive length even when the supplied distance lower bound is zero. The susceptibility series instead retains the root contribution one. These series are valid at all parameters, with infinity allowed; the geometric closed forms require the strict condition r<1. Natural subtraction handles degrees zero and one, and the real-subtraction theorem treats zero degree separately. The source witnesses include loops only on a degree-zero host, probability-one edges on a degree-one host, and the numerical d=2,p=1/4 case. The lattice specialization assumes local finiteness and a degree bound; the independent lattice geometry inventory supplies those facts mathematically.
