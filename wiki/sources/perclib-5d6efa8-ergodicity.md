## Hypotheses and reuse

The two general product modules allow arbitrary coordinate types and endpoint probabilities. Measurability is explicit. The finite-modification premise is a pointwise equivalence under insertion, not the one-sided condition defining an increasing event. Erasing a finite coordinate set proves membership in the sigma-algebra of the remaining coordinates. This does not itself establish measurability of a graph event on an uncountable vertex set.

The approximation lemma needs only a finite measure, not a product law. The cylinder factorization needs product independence and disjoint determining sets, but not invariance of the profile. Mixing additionally needs preservation of the law and eventual separation for each fixed pair of finite sets. Its sequence of relabellings need not arise from powers of a transformation or from a group action. The point-collision condition is sufficient, with a finite union of exceptional indices proving finite-set separation.

Event invariance is exact inverse-image invariance for each relabelling. Observable invariance is pointwise, with measurability and the countable target of extended natural numbers. Infinity is an allowed almost-sure value, and no moment assumption is made. The scalar square-identity helper excludes infinity; probabilities satisfy that exclusion automatically.

DK14, D1CH, MJD2, DRQ2 and WUAF identify the new grouped assertions. H0OC remains the graph zero-one statement, VEUZ supplies profile-preserving transport, and 6GNC retains the existing lattice cluster-count argument. The non-loop lattice specialization checks the mixing hypotheses without imposing translation invariance on unused diagonal coordinates. Almost-sure constancy by itself neither excludes a finite cluster count greater than one nor proves uniqueness.

The bond module assumes only an infinite additive group, not an Abelian or countable group. Right translations preserve the law when the profile is invariant on all pairs, including loops. The article proves mixing along distinct translations as a consequence of the general theorem, and separately uses diagonal erasure for loop-insensitive graph observables under off-diagonal invariance. Measurability remains a hypothesis on uncountable groups. The named integer witness is a statement about some raw pair being open, not an infinite-cluster event.

The source has concrete shift and finite-modification witnesses, including the event that infinitely many coordinates of the natural numbers are open. These are excluded from the named-theorem count. The new article gives the general proofs and the lattice specialization; it introduces no published-source diagnosis. Existing errata are unchanged.
