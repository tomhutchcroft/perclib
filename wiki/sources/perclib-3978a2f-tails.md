## Scope boundaries

The discrete identities use natural-valued or infinite X pointwise and allow an arbitrary measure; neither probability normalization nor a finite moment is needed. The power formula starts at k=1. The product identities instead use nonnegative real-valued observables, which are finite at each point, although their integrals can be infinite. The two-dimensional source statements assume S-finiteness and retain a measurable restricting event C. They assume the level-set inequalities that they integrate: they do not themselves prove a two-cluster BK statement or a Harris inequality for a particular model.

The square-root Laplace theorem is an order comparison, not an exact tail equivalent or a local limit theorem. It allows infinite values of T in its hypotheses; its vanishing upper bound then implies almost-sure finiteness. The source constructs a probability law with generating function 1-sqrt(1-z) to witness a nonzero limiting coefficient. Establishing that asymptotic for a branching process is separate.

The escape-to-volume estimate needs only finite windows, not nested windows or a geometric metric. Outside the countable setting its event bounds can be read as outer-measure bounds; finite-window volume is measurable from the membership hypotheses. The logarithmic consequence assumes an existing positive finite exponent and does not establish its existence. The integrated-to-pointwise lower bound requires both its integrated lower estimate and its pointwise upper estimate; neither is supplied by the sequence lemma itself.
