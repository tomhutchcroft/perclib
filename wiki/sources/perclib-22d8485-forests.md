## Definition and host conventions

## Finite and infinite operations

The unique-path event identity is deterministic and needs no countability. Countability enters the source's path probabilities and susceptibility sum, and supplies simultaneous absence of all zero-probability coordinates. The disconnected-host zero-measure lemma instead starts with almost-sure graph containment and needs no countability. The prescribed acyclic host need not coincide with positive support; zero-probability path edges are allowed. Empty paths have product one, and the ray's endpoint formula includes both zero and one parameters.

The degree identity is signed. An isolated vertex contributes minus two and is not a degree-one leaf. Component regrouping applies to arbitrary additive commutative monoids, and the degree of a vertex is unchanged on restriction to its component. These facts prove the forest inequality from the tree inequality. The article additionally retains twice the number of non-singleton components in the bound, an explicit consequence of the signed identity. The source witnesses include the empty forest, singleton tree, stars with at least three leaves, and the three-vertex path. There is no probabilistic encounter-point construction in this module, nor a long-range uniqueness proof.

Diagonal erasure uses pushforwards, not conditioning on all diagonal coordinates being closed. The general observable statement allows arbitrary vertices and any measurable target. Countability is needed for the source's cluster specializations. The opposite diagonal endpoints on two vertices and infinite susceptibility on the all-open countable graph are included witnesses.
