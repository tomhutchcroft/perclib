## Hypotheses and additional deductions

The partition assertions and the insertion-path classification work on arbitrary vertex sets. Root sums, component counts and their expectation formulas are stated for finite vertices. Every component has positive size; the empty vertex set instead has no components and an empty sum. Repeated-pair coordinates do not affect the simple graph.

0N0J and TUD2 retain the partition and root-sum identities. Their grouping proof applies to natural and real sums and to the finite induced graph. XCGS retains the averaged susceptibility identity. The new tag ZAIH records reciprocal-size counting and its expected value; its argument applies to any probability law on the finite configuration space, while the source exports the product-law version. No root-transitivity hypothesis is required, and the expectation of a reciprocal is not replaced by a reciprocal expectation.

NW8B groups the insertion and monotonicity arguments. The source's finite count decrease is at most one; the article also proves the exact decrement, determined by whether the two endpoints were already connected. The source's product comparison assumes order on all pairs; diagonal erasure gives the additional off-diagonal-only version. The path classification is explained by a simple path traversing the inserted pair at most once. Neither this deterministic statement nor the root-sum identities assume independence.

The natural-valued count in these contracts is used only for finite vertex sets; it is not extended to an infinite graph by treating an infinite number of components as a finite cardinal. Components of an induced domain are also distinct from full clusters intersecting that domain. The source witnesses include empty graphs, isolated vertices, a fully open three-vertex graph and constant endpoint profiles. All prior assertions, source records and errata are preserved. The inventory adds no compiled association or independent correspondence verdict.
