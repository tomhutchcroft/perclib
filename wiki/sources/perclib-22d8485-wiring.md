## Exploration and finite-volume cancellation

The first-exit source uses independence of three deterministic coordinate blocks after fixing the internal cluster. It does not condition by dividing by a cluster probability. The last edge leaving that cluster lands outside the original internal domain, and the rest of the path avoids the cluster. This differs from the article's equally valid BK decomposition at the first exit from the domain. The source's finite-domain intermediate result also covers a target inside the domain which is not internally connected to the root. This stronger event decomposition supplies the susceptibility recursion.

The contraction argument applies that recursion in a finite induced domain. Its supremum of internal susceptibilities is bounded by the domain cardinality, so subtraction of the contraction term is legitimate. Truncating a local domain can expose extra boundary pairs, but those leaving the finite ambient domain have zero remaining-connection probability. The argument does not use an unsupported monotonicity of the boundary functional under shrinking the domain. Exhaustion passes the uniform bound to full susceptibility. Infinite B is allowed in the inequality; finite B is required for the almost-sure conclusion. Anonymous examples cover a two-vertex half-open profile and the zero profile at a=0.

## The collapsed law and derivative hypotheses

The collapse proof uses disjoint fibres of the map on unordered pairs. Each fibre is a countable coordinate block; its all-closed probability is computed by the infinite product of exponentials, using summability. Independence of the blocks and the finite-cylinder characterization of the product measure identify the full pushed-forward law. General collapse may identify disconnected vertices. The ghost connection detects escape exactly, whereas preservation of an interior-to-interior connection requires that the starting collapsed cluster avoid the ghost.

The main derivative exports assume countable vertices and summability of every intensity row, including its diagonal term. Since every diagonal intensity is a finite nonnegative real, this is equivalent to off-diagonal row summability and puts no restriction on its value. For a fixed finite domain, only the rows at its vertices enter the proof; the source retains the global hypothesis. No finite range, transitivity or finite susceptibility is assumed.

On the finite ghost graph, state-independent pivotality factors from the tested pair's state. Its closing probability absorbs Russo's exponential coefficient. On each unexplored-set event a boundary pair has a unique inside/outside orientation, so there is no factor of two. The factorization of the original unexplored-set event from internal pairs translates the derivative back to the original graph. The probability-weighted boundary inequality then uses summability and q <= beta J. Taking a minimum is legitimate because the root singleton belongs to the finite family.

The derivative within the nonnegative half-line includes the right derivative at zero. The ordinary derivative statement requires strictly positive beta because the source extends parameters using their nonnegative part. In the appended two-vertex witness, escape is 1−exp(−beta), and the exact derivative is evaluated as exp(−beta). At zero the right derivative is one while the extension's left derivative is zero. The append does not change any of the preceding 16 public contracts.
