## Scope, generated versions and witnesses

The six mass-transport declarations have the same additive counterparts: replace multiplication by addition, the identity by zero and inversion by negation. This includes pair relabelling and preservation of the full configuration law, as well as the three integral statements. Deterministic inversion permits arbitrary groups and arbitrary nonnegative extended sums; the random statements use countability to exchange the integral with the sums. For the full bond law, profile invariance includes diagonal coordinates. The graph-only off-diagonal variant and the cluster applications in the article are separate deductions, not additional exports of this module.

The covariance identity needs neither increasingness nor valid probability weights as an algebraic statement. Nonnegative covariance needs both increasingness and a profile in the probability cube. The intensity formula excludes zero-intensity coordinates from its coefficient sum and assumes positive β before dividing. It supplies no decision-tree or OSSS inequality; the article's revealment conversion retains that separate hypothesis.

Variance tensorization includes the closed probability cube. The derivative of the event polynomial exists at every real parameter, but the source's clamped probability family is differentiated only in the open interval. Odds comparisons require a nonempty proper event and interior parameters. The squared-increment bound and zero-increment argument remain finite-dimensional; no infinite-coordinate theorem is inferred from the pointwise forcing identities.

The variance source supplies anonymous witnesses at deterministic profiles and at a one-coordinate increasing event: the variance and derivative bounds are equalities and the normalized odds ratio is constant. The covariance source has the one-coordinate identity q(1−q) and the coefficient bound at J = β = 1. The mass-transport source sends one unit from each integer to its successor, both deterministically and under a constant-profile bond law. These examples are checked source witnesses, not additional named rows. The article's total-mass-two example and its negative variance polynomial at q = 2 are explanatory deductions.
