## Scope of affinity

The measure identities hold on arbitrary index sets and at deterministic parameter endpoints. They follow by checking finite all-open cylinders, which form a generating pi-system. There is no division by an endpoint probability and no conditioning on a null event.

The real affine evaluation at line 150 has no measurability hypothesis: measure evaluation there is the outer measure of an arbitrary subset. In contrast, identifying the endpoint law with the probability of a forcing preimage uses a measurable event, as do the signed slope and pivotal formulas. AQDI distinguishes the measure-level identity from these event formulas. Finite dependence is unnecessary when exactly one coordinate varies; it remains necessary for the earlier finite-cylinder derivative argument unless a separate infinite-dimensional limiting argument is supplied.

The bond probability application uses countable vertices to obtain connection measurability. The diagonal-pivotal event is empty by a deterministic equality of the two simple open graphs, and needs no countability. Self-connection likewise has zero slope. The source's two-coordinate conjunction illustrates the formula; a one-edge graph gives unit connection slope. The article's endpoint derivatives, unit Lipschitz consequence and infinite-open-set counterexample are additional deductions, not extra source exports.

The finite-product update lemma at line 61 is the cylinder calculation used in the proof, not a separate probabilistic hypothesis. The article keeps the state-independent pivotal event distinct from a closed-pivotal event. No simultaneous infinite-coordinate Russo sum or new published-source erratum is claimed.
