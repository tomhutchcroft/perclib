## The diagonal input

9L60 separates the first-step term whose intermediate vertex equals the target. That term contains a return weight and is bounded by a(x,y)m^(n+1). Only the remaining terms use the off-diagonal induction hypothesis. This gives the coefficient recurrence and the additional geometric factor, without extending the off-diagonal convolution inequality to a diagonal pair.

The abstract source induction assumes only identity initialization, first-step recursion, the diagonal estimate and the off-diagonal convolution inequality. In its application, endpoint sequence sums provide the initialization and recursion; the row bound supplies diagonal control because an endpoint sum is at most the unrestricted walk sum. The article states this distinction explicitly. No countability or symmetry is needed for these deterministic extended sums.

The simple-path comparison is an injection: the continuation list together with the fixed root recovers the path and preserves its endpoint. The product law applies to distinct edges of a simple path, not to every repeated-edge sequence in the majorant. Countability enters the connection-probability application. The original kernel may have arbitrary diagonal entries because that application removes them.

All coefficients and sums are extended nonnegative, including K,m at zero, one or infinity. The geometric-factor notation H(t) means sum(n>=0) t^n: finite reciprocal below one, infinity at or above one. The product convention 0 times infinity equal to zero is retained. Finite denominators are a specialization of the source statement, not extra hypotheses silently added to it. The original row and doubling-convolution assertions and all errata remain unchanged.
