## Quantifiers and model inputs

The negligibility proof chooses the truncation after fixing the terminal radius and then holds that number fixed throughout the backward integration. No joint exceptional null set over all truncations is needed. The probability-measure theorem writes the real first and second moments as Bochner integrals; its strictly positive second-moment lower bound excludes the totalized zero value of a nonintegrable integrand. Positive truncations and integrable first moments likewise force nonnegative support, as explained in the article. Its two-point witness supplies the analytic assumptions without realizing a percolation model.

The quadratic-recursion source proves a fixed-lambda transform limit. It does not construct a branching process, identify its offspring law, prove extinction, or invoke a continuity theorem to obtain weak convergence. Its rational-map examples verify the positive orbit and triangular-array hypotheses. The full branching interpretation and convergence of conditional means remain separate parts of the article.

The amplitude source uses an arbitrary countable bond profile at the endpoint. Lower semicontinuity, rather than parameter monotonicity, is enough to carry a uniform upper bound to that endpoint. It assumes the two-scale inequality and finite susceptibility below the endpoint; it neither derives them from a long-range kernel nor identifies the endpoint as critical. The one-vertex example checks the theorem's hypotheses, not the spatial mechanism in the power-law paper. All three headers identify the proofs as new project work; the amplitude file cites arXiv:2008.11197v2, section 4, as motivation.
