## Scope of the arguments

Harris uses arbitrary coordinate index types and arbitrary coordinate probability measures. Linear order is required for the association theorems; preorders suffice for monotonicity of averaging, and the splicing, dependence and contraction facts use no order. The source does not require the order to generate the sigma-algebra. Its finite-dimensional function theorem uses a common finite support and uniform bounds, while its general theorem applies to increasing measurable events without finite dependence. The approximation quantifier is over every finite superset of a suitable finite set, not just one chosen sequence.

Several averaging helpers are stronger as formal total-integral identities than their ordinary integration reading. The measurability result at line 227 needs measurable input but no integrability; the norm bound at line 233 has no measurability hypothesis; the dependence identity at line 241 needs neither function measurability nor coordinate probability laws. The mean identity at line 199 instead assumes integrability and uses almost-everywhere integrable sections. The article states measurable/integrable or bounded specializations when assigning ordinary probabilistic meanings to these expressions. The fixed-function identity at line 250 is pointwise because every relevant section is constant.

Finite open witnesses supply a separate route to measurability on countable coordinates. The deterministic witness representations do not need countability, but an arbitrary uncountable union of cylinders cannot be declared measurable from that representation alone. Connection and escape have finite path witnesses on arbitrary vertices; the probability applications here use countable vertices. Escaped sets may be infinite. The root-infinite-cluster event is increasing and measurable on countable vertices, but it is not generally a finite-open-witness event. The article records its countable intersection of escape events separately.

The finite FKG/Holley exports allow dependent probability measures and zero configuration masses. The FKG function export assumes nonnegative real observables; the article obtains signed functions by shifting constants on the finite space. The Holley measure export states increasing-event domination; the article also proves increasing-observable domination from four functions. These additional deductions are identified in TKDC and KB49. Probability normalization cannot be dropped without the corresponding total-mass factors.

The mass-conversion helper at FKG line 47 explicitly omits the finite-index hypothesis: for any finite measure its singleton outer-mass inequality passes to real values. It does not establish measurability of arbitrary-index singleton sets or an infinite-space FKG theorem. The event-sum formula at line 36 and the FKG/Holley measure conclusions themselves retain finite configuration spaces. The article uses their usual finite probability interpretation.

EZJ7 proves the finite Boolean-lattice four-functions theorem with nonnegative finite real functions; no monotonicity, positivity or normalization is needed. The proof handles a vanishing cross coefficient before cancellation. This is an imported theorem from the pinned mathlib file, not another named PercLib export, and is excluded from the 47-row census. General distributive lattices and arbitrary subfamilies in that mathlib file are outside the article statement. The product-mass lattice equality and Holley comparison are verified coordinatewise, with endpoint parameters allowed.

Source witnesses include inhomogeneous deterministic coordinates, empty/full events, and the event that every coordinate of a countable product is open. The latter is measurable and increasing but cannot be forced by any finite open set. The open-event module includes a genuine two-vertex connection and a configuration escaping a singleton despite having a finite cluster. The finite FKG module recovers finite product Harris and ordered-profile comparison. The two dependent two-coordinate laws in the article are additional examples separating association from independence and showing failure of the lattice condition.
