## Hypotheses and reuse

Vertex-bijection transport is deterministic before a law is specified. Its probability consequences assume an explicit equality of transported laws. The measurable equivalence gives an equality of outer measures on arbitrary sets and of lower integrals for arbitrary nonnegative extended-valued functions. Thus none of the 23 transport contracts assumes countability. The article identifies the ordinary event-probability and expectation interpretation separately; it does not infer that graph observables are measurable on an arbitrary uncountable vertex set.

Configuration pullback is measurable for every vertex map. Injectivity is needed for the product-law identification and for preserving the size and infinitude of an image cluster. The three embedding comparisons assume countability only of the source vertex type. This supplies source-event and volume measurability, while the target side can still be interpreted by outer measure and lower integral. The domination hypothesis excludes diagonal pairs; erasing the source diagonal supplies the full-coordinate inequality used in the coupling. No finite row, positive bond probability or finite susceptibility is assumed.

The closed-part result requires actual absence of open boundary pairs and is valid without countability. The product-law application to a disjoint union uses countability of both parts to exclude all cross pairs on one null set. Independence of internal restrictions is a different assertion: it holds for arbitrary vertex types and arbitrary cross probabilities because the determining pair coordinates are disjoint. It cannot by itself identify restricted clusters with full clusters.

ZE95 groups exact transport. 1H8U gives the embedding comparisons, 7CR8 the closed-part identity and WD2J the countable disjoint-union consequences. Coordinate sampling reuses YMHY, measurable bond transport reuses 6VY0, diagonal insensitivity reuses EYCQ and internal independence reuses RDX7. The source exports the within-side two-point and susceptibility identities only for the first inclusion; the article's second-side versions are additional symmetric deductions. The finite-box interpretation and the distinction from noninjective identifications are explanatory consequences. No duplicate independence assertion is allocated.

The empty and singleton parts, repeated pairs, endpoint profiles and infinite susceptibilities are retained. The source includes identity, constant-profile and opposite-diagonal witnesses; anonymous examples are excluded from the census. No new published-source diagnosis is made. Existing errata remain unchanged, and source-contract correspondence remains pending independent review and compiled association.
