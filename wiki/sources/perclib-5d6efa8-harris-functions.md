## Measure and order assumptions

The coordinate set is arbitrary in every Bernoulli-product theorem. Event and function measurability remain explicit; monotonicity alone is not used to establish measurability on the configuration space. Zero and one coordinate probabilities are allowed. The two monotonicity helpers work for any preordered target, while the integration theorems use extended nonnegative or real targets.

The layer-cake integral identities are proved for an s-finite measure, a countable sum of finite measures, on an arbitrary measurable space. They do not assume a probability law or an integrable observable. The level-measure measurability helper is broader still: antitonicity gives measurability in the real level for any measure and any function, with outer measure on nonmeasurable superlevel sets. It does not prove measurability of the underlying function or justify an integral identity without the other hypotheses.

The extended inequalities allow infinite expectations and use the nonnegative convention zero times infinity equals zero. The signed-real statements instead assume a common finite absolute bound. The article obtains that common bound from boundedness of the two functions, shifts them to nonnegative functions and cancels only finite terms. It does not subtract infinite expectations or assert covariance where it is undefined. The earlier ordered-product assertion covers finite-coordinate bounded observables; the new Bernoulli function forms remove that finite-dependence restriction.

Complementation transports the profile to one minus the original profile; it is not generally an invariance at the original profile. The mixed event inequality is negative correlation of one increasing and one decreasing event, not negative association of arbitrary events and not independence after conditioning.
