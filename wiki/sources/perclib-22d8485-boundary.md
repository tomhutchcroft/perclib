## Scope, finite values and the correction record

The deterministic deletion inclusion and the upper probability bound allow arbitrary S and arbitrary endpoints. In particular, the formal statement is broader than the source docstring's opening phrase restricting endpoints to the complement. Deleting an endpoint makes the restricted connection event empty and leaves the corresponding self-connection term available in the error sum. Countability enters the probability upper bound, not the deterministic path decomposition or the probability comparison from event inclusion. The deleted set need not be finite. Finite S is used in the subsequent boundary sums.

Last exit is obtained from the existing first-exit theorem applied from the outside with domain S-complement. No new proof of that imported theorem is claimed in this inventory. The deleted factor concerns connection inside the complement; the internal factor is unrestricted full connection. The boundary-row inequality uses only an off-diagonal intensity row bound of one and remains an extended inequality even if maximum susceptibility is infinite. The real quotient theorem additionally requires a finite uniform susceptibility bound, which makes all relevant boundary rows and outgoing sums finite. Diagonal intensities are unrestricted throughout these graph statements.

The two auxiliary real-value identities should not be used to infer convergence without their required bounds. The first has no finite-total premise: both the extended-to-real map at infinity and a nonsummable nonnegative real series use totalized zero values. The second has finite individual deleted susceptibilities but no row-summability premise; its weighted series can still be nonsummable. In the application, the row bound and finite maximum susceptibility establish actual finiteness before the real inequality is used.

The source allows beta = 0 and empty S, whereas the wiki states the informative quotient for positive beta and nonempty S. The source explicitly splits a zero denominator from a positive one and uses real division by zero equals zero in the former case. This is not a probability law at infinite intensity or a positive-denominator estimate at zero. The numerator comparison is separately exported for every nonnegative real denominator and every profile with uniformly finite susceptibility.

The counterexample uses finite nonnegative weights 99/100 and 1/100 on the three-vertex path, zero on its missing proper pair, and unit intensity. Its off-diagonal rows are 99/100, 1 and 1/100; no diagonal hypothesis is needed. The explicit symmetric-kernel witness confirms these assumptions. A finite exponential-series estimate gives p > 3/5, and strict convexity gives 0 < t < 1/100. The resulting rational lower bound 841/10050 for the unsigned-square expression exceeds the exact boundary functional 1/100. No decimal approximation is used. The boundary-functional identity itself does not need the prescribed internal edge weight.

The boundary module has witnesses for deleting the empty set, deleting a middle vertex in a three-vertex profile, and applying the corrected bound to a unit edge on two vertices at every finite intensity. The middle-vertex example is valid for every profile on those three vertices; its formal statement does not impose path support despite the example's docstring. The two-vertex witness supplies normalized rows and susceptibility at most two.
