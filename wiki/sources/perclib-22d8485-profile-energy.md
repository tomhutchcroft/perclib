## Overwrites, conditioning and positive probability

The overwrite identity holds for arbitrary deterministic blocks on arbitrary coordinate sets. It is a pushforward law, not conditioning on an assignment of positive probability. Finite assignment factorization needs a measurable outside event and pointwise determination by the complement; the source reduces this to block independence. The strict-interior hypothesis in its positivity theorem ensures every finite assignment has positive weight. The article's finite-image bound uses the weaker assignment-specific condition and is explicitly an additional deduction. Measurability of that image follows from a finite union of measurable sections, not a general assertion about images of measurable sets.

The source includes mixed deterministic endpoints, the empty block, a positive two-coordinate assignment and a diagonal closed-bond witness. ## Finite changes and divergent rows

The single-pair cluster containment uses three old clusters as a convenient upper bound: those of the root and the two endpoints. Inserting a pair actually merges at most the two endpoint components. The deterministic statements need neither local finiteness nor countability. Countability is used to make the union over infinite-root events measurable. The source includes the empty vertex type, whose global event is empty; H0OC retains its existing nonempty formulation. The zero-profile and all-open profile on natural vertices witness both probabilities. A rooted infinite-cluster event is not in general a tail event.

InfiniteDegree instead gives a condition forcing a specified rooted event to have probability one. Its Borel–Cantelli proof uses independent distinct incident pairs; XJ74 already proves the same conclusion through generating functions. The extended-sum truncation lemma assumes every term is finite, and the intensity comparison requires positive finite intensity. It implies zero thresholds from a single divergent row, without transitivity or a condition on every root. The constant-row witness is deterministic and allows an arbitrary infinite index type. The finite positive-intensity conclusions are not claims about a law at infinite parameter.

## The topology and the fixed host

Finite-coordinate determination automatically makes the event a finite union of cylinder atoms, so its continuity theorem needs no additional measurability assumption. The finite-domain connection proof reduces to those atoms. A countable finite exhaustion includes the empty type; lower semicontinuity comes from an increasing union of internal connections.

For the infinite-cluster upper bound, a common locally finite host makes the incident edge set of each finite domain finite. The first-exit path argument is deterministic; simultaneous suppression of all off-host non-diagonal pairs uses countability. A root outside the finite domain escapes by its trivial path. The conclusion is upper semicontinuity on the supported-profile subspace, not on all profiles. Diagonal coordinates are unrestricted and no uniform degree bound is imposed. The source's finite three-vertex host and homogeneous family on the integers supply non-vacuity examples for the upper and lower statements respectively.

The source's parameter type is any topological preordered space; its one-sided statements are continuity within an order interval. On a real interval they give the left continuity of the two-point function and the right continuity of the infinite-cluster probability under the stated fixed-host hypothesis. There is no missing implication from one side to the other. The article's translation-invariant long-range example demonstrates why the host restriction cannot be dropped.
