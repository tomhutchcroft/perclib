## Definitions, domain hypotheses and witnesses

Russo defines finite weights (line 30), their expectation sum (35), deleted weights (38), signed deleted weights (41), coordinate forcing (45), forced differences (49), flips (52), the flip permutation (61), the open-set encoding (184), event indicators (187) and positive pivotality (191). These are the algebra and conventions used in 71GK, 7WIO and their proofs, not a second product measure. The canonical finite integral is identified with the sum before the derivative is interpreted probabilistically.

The polynomial results allow every real profile. Their intensity substitutions likewise allow real intensities and real parameters; nonnegative intensities and parameters are a sufficient condition for a probability profile, not hypotheses of the algebraic identities. The domain-transport results instead take a supplied unit-interval-valued profile and require agreement with real coordinate extensions on the parameter set and separately at the base point. In the cylinder and finite-support versions, only coordinates of the fixed finite support require this agreement. Coordinates outside it can have arbitrary parameter dependence.

The pointwise derivative theorems assume differentiability of the real coordinate curves at the base point, not global continuous differentiability. The integrated theorem in the [pivotal/Russo inventory](sources/perclib-5d6efa8-pivotal-russo.md) has the stronger continuously differentiable-curve hypothesis. The within-domain predicate in the source permits arbitrary parameter sets and does not require the base point to lie in their accumulation set. At an isolated point it does not determine a unique derivative. The article deliberately imposes accumulation for its limit interpretation and uses the right derivative at zero or left derivative at one for nontrivial probability intervals.

The general event derivative retains a signed forced difference. Increasingness identifies that difference with positive pivotality. This identification is itself valid for arbitrary increasing measurable events on arbitrary ambient index sets; finite support is needed for the derivative sum here, not for that measurable-event identity. Finite dependence implies event measurability and is inherited by the pivotal test on the support. Outside the support both forced tests agree.

The source witnesses include the empty coordinate set, the zero observable, a singleton opening indicator with polynomial expectation equal to its parameter, intensity derivative J at zero, and a mixed endpoint profile with one coordinate increasing while the other decreases. The cylinder witnesses include an empty block and the opening and closing events for one coordinate of a countably infinite product, with derivatives one and minus one at the interval endpoints. The finite-support witness uses one coordinate of that product at unit intensity. None provides a passage from finite-support derivatives to an infinite pivotal series.
