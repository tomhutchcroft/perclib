## Scope boundaries

The offspring source assumes an existing probability mass function and states the mean-one hypothesis as an extended nonnegative sum. Its second-order expansion assumes convergence of the real second factorial-moment series, which equals the variance under mean one. The termwise cubic estimate establishes a limit for each fixed offspring count; domination uses only the quadratic factorial weight, so no third moment is required. Existence and uniqueness of the root at s < 1 also hold for deterministic-one offspring, but convergence of that root to one excludes this law. The explicit critical-binary witness checks the nondegenerate hypotheses and the analytic asymptotic, not a construction of a random tree.

The size-bias source has two different levels of statement. Its raw change-of-measure identities use total real integration and total division, without an integrability or positive-mean assumption. They must not be described on their own as normalized probability constructions. The limit theorems separately assume that the weighted pushforwards equal given probability measures. The article's positive finite mean is the standard probabilistic hypothesis ensuring that normalization. The fields and scales are strictly positive for the varying-parameter Laplace limit, whose target coefficient is also strictly positive.

The positive tail threshold stays fixed and must be a non-atom of the limiting law. No such exclusion is needed at zero for the Laplace kernel, which has a continuous filled-in value there. Neither theorem permits a simultaneous limit in which the threshold approaches zero or the Laplace parameter tends to infinity. The spatial escape example concerns finite measures tested against bounded continuous functions, not a percolation construction or a substitute for spatial tightness of a cluster law.
