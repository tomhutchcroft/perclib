## Exponential endpoints and source scope

The source's scale chi is positive but is not defined to equal the mean. The first moment inequality gives an upper bound by chi. Its series expansion and weighted bound work for any measure; probability normalization is needed for the ordinary exponential's constant term and for the tail bound by one. The variables are real valued and nonnegative everywhere. Applying these analytic theorems to an extended cluster volume requires its almost-sure finiteness and a real-valued representative, as well as the moment hypotheses.

The weighted series has radius endpoint z=1 where its proposed bound is singular. The ordinary series uses positive-degree coefficients for 1−sqrt(1−z). Each finite polynomial is bounded at z=1 by continuity from below, then the extended sum is bounded by taking the supremum. Thus the ordinary endpoint is included without asserting a finite weighted moment there. The point-mass example verifies both the endpoint ordinary bound and the tail estimate at n=chi for chi >= 1. The paper's endpoint correction still needs its prefactor or eventual threshold and retains its provisional status.

## Extended identities and normalization

The size-bias module uses an arbitrary base measure and extended nonnegative variables. The density integral identity itself does not require a finite positive mean. Only under that hypothesis does it describe a probability law. Its cluster instance uses the root's inclusion for positivity, and requires finite susceptibility for normalization. Infinite second moments are allowed. The singleton cluster and the uniform law on {1,2} provide the anonymous witnesses; the latter assigns size-biased mass 2/3 to the value two.

The truncation source allows zero finite first moment, infinite second moment, and infinite lambda or threshold. The proof deals with a zero mean or infinite threshold before cancellation. If the mean is positive, an infinite second moment forces an infinite threshold, so the general statement does not claim a useful finite truncation in that case. The article's finite formulation is a specialization. Parameter continuity and critical interpolation are not proved by these contracts.
