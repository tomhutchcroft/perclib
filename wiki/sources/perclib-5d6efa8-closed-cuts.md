## Scope of the finite witness

The theorem at line 64 returns a finite **vertex** set whose entire pair boundary is closed. It does not return a finite set of pair coordinates. The article retains that statement, then separately proves a finite coordinate certificate when configurations are restricted to a locally finite host. In the unrestricted infinite complete host, even the empty configuration has no finite closed-coordinate certificate for nonconnection of two distinct vertices: an unused intermediate vertex permits a two-edge connection agreeing with all inspected coordinates. This is a distinction between two certificate notions, not a counterexample to the exported theorem or an erratum in a published paper.

The closed-pair characterization quantifies over complete-graph walks, including the empty walk when the endpoints coincide. Loop coordinates cannot meet such a walk. The prescribed-set containment equivalence retains the hypothesis that the root lies in the set. Its right-hand condition is the source's closure of all pairs from the full root cluster to the complement of the prescribed set.

Decreasingness needs no countability. The article additionally explains measurability on countable vertices through the usual product sigma-algebra. The source witnesses cover the empty configuration with distinct vertices and the fully open two-vertex configuration.
