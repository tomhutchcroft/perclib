## Scope of the normalization and examples

The normalized matrix estimate needs row bounds, not symmetry or a constant row sum. Its percolation specialization requires countable vertices but not transitivity. Exact normalization is needed for the susceptibility lower bound; the triangle upper bound only uses rows at most one. This file does not prove the locally Lipschitz and almost-everywhere derivative part of 1EWX; that belongs to the separate susceptibility-regularity argument already inventoried.

The line source proves the full explicit MTYL calculation and strengthens its eventual inequality to the interval (0,1/48). Its formulas are finite for every finite intensity. The ordinary derivative statement excludes zero because the implemented real extension clamps negative intensities. The explicit example at 1/100 supplies a model instance, not merely a numerical evaluation. The related published-paper erratum remains provisional; a formal counterexample to the stronger expression does not prove a replacement differential inequality or its endpoint applications.

The AHY3 example is a countable disjoint union, with uniformly bounded degree and edge intensity. It distinguishes the infimum over rooted thresholds from a minimum, without contradicting the equality of rooted thresholds on a connected positive support. The component restriction is justified almost surely by the zero probabilities of all non-host edges, not for every ambient configuration. Private helpers implement this restriction and pullback of the bond law. The constructor witnesses that the kernel equations are satisfiable. All three headers identify the proofs as new project work.
