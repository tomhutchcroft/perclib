## Definitions, helpers and scope

The nine explicit named definitions in Basic start at lines 60, 65, 69, 73, 77, 85, 214, 219 and 236. They define the open graph, site cluster, connection and connection-to-set events, product law, general rooted percolation probability, lattice percolation probability, capped real critical probability and its unit-interval representative. The lattice/site-configuration abbreviations and probability-measure instance are not included in the theorem count. The existing 8FLS and 8EIH explain these conventions, with general theta and connection-to-set notation supplied in the surrounding prose. In particular, evaluating theta at the threshold neither proves nor assumes its vanishing there.

The deterministic foundational and Harris contracts need no countability. The two self/adjacent probability identities are finite-cylinder facts and likewise hold for arbitrary vertex sets. The general connection and infinite-cluster measurability proofs in Basic, and the Harris/coupling applications as exported here, impose countability. Fixed-list cylinders and the witness/escape set identities do not. The article retains these different scopes.

For SelfAvoiding, a specified root in a locally finite graph lies in a countable host component even if the whole vertex set is uncountable. Its finite-length open-path events are finite unions of cylinders. Their intersection is the infinite-cluster event, so the rootwise probability bound requires no global countability. Only the simultaneous all-root corollary adds that hypothesis. The proof does not take an uncountable union of null events.

The path count excludes immediate backtracking, but the opening probability uses all distinct vertices, including the root. At n edges the uniform exponent is n+1; in the source's shifted notation it is n+2 at n+1 edges. The root-dependent refinement w(x)*Delta*b^(n-1)*p^n is an additional deduction from the same cylinder products, not the exact exported uniform bound. Length zero requires the root open. Natural b=max(Delta-1,0) permits degrees zero and one at p=1; no equality case is inferred at b*p=1 for larger degree.

The private lattice lemmas at lines 141 and 155 cover every neighbour by a signed coordinate step and furnish a local degree bound of 2d without adding a global local-finiteness instance. MIUX uses precisely that geometric bound. Anonymous source examples check the open root at path length zero, degree-zero and degree-one graphs at p=1, the one-dimensional threshold equal to one, and zero percolation probability at p=1/4 in dimension two. The article's theta_s(1)=1 on the line is an additional deduction from countably many probability-one open vertices; the source exports no critical-vanishing theorem. The zero-dimensional singleton has the same capped threshold but no infinite cluster at any parameter.
