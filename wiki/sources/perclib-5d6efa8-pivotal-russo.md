## Scope and endpoints

The connection-pivotal statements are deterministic on arbitrary vertex sets. The insertion decomposition does not assume distinct endpoints of the inserted pair; the pivotal characterization assumes it is not diagonal. Every-pair and cardinality bounds concern a realized connection: deleting a pivotal pair destroys that existing connection, so every current open walk must use it. The distance is the distance in the open graph, not a host distance. Closed configurations can have infinitely many positive pivotal pairs; no unconditional cardinality bound is inferred. Domain restriction commutes with forcing an internal pair, and noninternal pairs cannot affect an internal event.

The algebraic module uses positive pivotality: forcing open succeeds and forcing closed fails. It is state independent for arbitrary events, and measurable when the event is measurable. For finite indices that hypothesis is automatic. Increasingness is needed for the event decomposition and for identifying the signed increment with the positive pivotal indicator. Disjointness of the two pieces, open-positive-pivotal inclusion in the event, and the positive-pivotal count identity restricted to the event hold without increasingness. XRSL keeps these scopes separate.

The finite open-coordinate weighted identity is algebraic at all real profiles for a flip-invariant real observable. At probability profiles it gives the finite open-pivotal factorization. XRSL's corresponding arbitrary-index probability statement is an additional deduction from FH29, not attributed to the finite RussoIntegral export. Its closed-pivotal factorization likewise follows from fixed-block independence, and explains precisely when the exponential coefficient may be absorbed into closed pivotality.

RussoIntegral has two private helpers at lines 23 and 29 identifying finite event/pivotal weighted sums with the real measure evaluations. The integrated theorem assumes globally differentiable real coordinate curves with continuous derivatives, and a supplied interval-valued profile agreeing with them on the unoriented closed interval. That probability profile can be arbitrary outside the interval; the real curve may leave the cube there. VYTC uses the same polynomial/FTC argument and oriented integral, including reversed or degenerate intervals. Its affine-segment specialization permits unordered profiles and retains signed coefficients. The homogeneous integral allows zero endpoints, whereas the open-pivotal derivative divided by p requires p>0. At p=1 the latter is a derivative within [0,1], interpreted from the left.

The finite-domain module allows arbitrary ambient vertices. Only the internal pair coordinates enter the derivative; the domain is finite, and the pair intensities are finite and nonnegative, with no ambient total-sum bound. The source uses the nonnegative-real conversion of a real beta and differentiates within [0,infinity); 6JBS gives the right derivative at zero. Diagonal terms vanish for every graph-determined event, not only connection. The derivative bound counts each non-diagonal unordered pair once, and the susceptibility derivative sums over the finitely many target vertices. The Lipschitz consequence is an additional deduction from the derivative bound; no interchange with an infinite-volume limit is claimed.

The source witnesses include reflexive connection with no pivotal pairs, the sole two-vertex bond pivotal in every configuration, diagonal nonpivotality, a one-coordinate indicator increment, zero-length integration intervals, empty/full events, a two-coordinate conjunction with probability p squared and derivative 2p, and finite-domain intensity formulas at all nonnegative beta.
