## Completed bootstrap arguments, 16 November 2026

This update leaves the historical passage maps and their pinned links above intact. The earlier bootstrap obligations are now supplied by the [spatial amplitude proof](../articles/finite-set-hyperscaling.md#section-spatial-bootstrap) (XHZD), [critical endpoint theorem](../articles/finite-set-hyperscaling.md#section-critical-powerlaw) (KXV3), [edge-weight threshold theorem](../articles/finite-set-hyperscaling.md#section-weight-threshold) (58BJ) and [elementary square-root contraction](../articles/finite-set-hyperscaling.md#section-elementary-bootstrap) (BWH0). These are prose proofs, not compiled correspondence certificates.

The argument normalizes the off-diagonal intensity row and rescales the percolation parameter; a diagonal kernel value creates no edge. Small intensities use the susceptibility row bound. In the positive-intensity host every finite positive parameter gives nondegenerate edge probabilities, so 75N0 suffices without the unresolved zero/one extension for general random environments. The lattice proof uses a genuine annulus of cardinality comparable to the d-th power of its outer radius, and applies UVQ6 to that same annulus. Integer radius rounding changes only the fixed constant.

Two intermediate powers in the source need attention. Theorem 3.1 has the amplitude squared, so Cauchy–Schwarz gives the amplitude itself; this still contracts because its exponent is below 2/(1+theta). In the weight-threshold proof the epsilon power remains minus (1 minus a)/2 through the scale choice. The [two new provisional errata](../errata/hutchcroft-powerlaw-2021.json) record the precise arXiv v2 locations, repairs and Lean consequences. They do not alter the final conclusions. A targeted publisher-HTML check, described below, finds both discrepancies there too; this is not a full edition-equivalence claim. The two older entries and their implementation-progress notes are preserved.

Sharpness supplies finite susceptibility below the critical parameter, including the initially possible infinite-threshold case. The best amplitude is a finite supremum, with no assumption that an integer attains it. The uniform tail forces a finite critical parameter by testing a fixed large finite star as intensity tends to infinity. At the resulting finite endpoint, each finite cluster-volume witness is already open at a smaller parameter in the common coupling. No continuity of the infinite-cluster event is assumed. Countability then passes root finiteness to absence of every infinite cluster.

The averaged lattice bound keeps the conversion from observation-set volume to radius: UVQ6 gives a radius exponent 2(d-alpha)/3. This implies the weaker displayed exponent 2(d-alpha)/(3d) in equation (1.3), while no pointwise decay is inferred. Proposition 1.4 is proved separately using finite invariant truncations of the intensity weights, the exponent-zero two-cluster estimate and the elementary truncated mean. Thus it does not assume a finite square-root sum of the intensity kernel, or use the improved finite-set hyperscaling input. The weaker numerical two-ghost constant is stated explicitly.

Still separate: the paper's exact numerical two-ghost constants, degenerate general random environments, the stronger infinite-graph combinatorial statement, bibliography, full journal-version comparison and independent correspondence review. The completed module census does not remove these source-text obligations.

The [group-kernel corollary](../articles/finite-set-hyperscaling.md#section-group-kernel) (S01U) now completes Remark 1.3 as well. It uses a symmetric enumeration kernel with exponent between one and two, discards the diagonal, verifies mass transport for the right-translation action and counts the edges above each threshold. It makes no finite-generation assumption and asserts no comparison to a finite-degree Cayley graph.

## Infinite-graph partition completed

The [finite-core extension](../articles/finite-set-hyperscaling.md#section-infinite-splitting) (RZSW) supplies the stronger locally finite infinite-graph statement of Lemma 2.4. Its statement and vertex-coverage clauses were checked in the publisher HTML, Section 2.1. A spanning tree has a finite subtree containing all marks; every complementary component attaches at exactly one already-covered core vertex. Adding that unmarked branch to an incident piece preserves the finite partition's marked counts, covers every vertex and, on a tree, partitions every edge. This proves the stronger combinatorial formulation without an infinite recursive splitting argument. The earlier finite-witness proof remains the route used for countable BK. ## The earlier incident-weight bound: normalization checked

The [normalization comparison](../articles/stopped-exploration.md#section-incident-normalization) reads the root-edge convention in Section 2.1 and the statement of Corollary 3.2 of [arXiv:2007.15625v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.15625v1#page=21), the earlier paper cited as reference 45. That root edge is sampled proportionally to its intensity. Expanding its expectation introduces the total incident intensity L, so the corresponding sum bound has right side 42 L divided by the square root of lambda. The power-law paper's equation (1.7), in both arXiv v2 and the publisher HTML, drops L without a unit-row assumption.

The [new provisional erratum](../errata/hutchcroft-powerlaw-2021.json) includes a nearest-neighbour integer-line witness and the corrected translation. The source application in Proposition 1.4 normalizes total kernel mass to one, making the off-diagonal incident row at most one. BWH0 normalizes that row directly and gives an independent route using a weaker constant. No main bootstrap conclusion changes. The quoted corollary remains an external theorem; checking its root-edge law and statement is not a full proof audit of the Ising paper. The earlier 37 provisional entries are preserved, giving 38 in total. General bibliography and full journal-edition reconciliation remain open.
