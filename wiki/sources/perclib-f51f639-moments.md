## Scope and provenance

The real-exponent ratio theorem takes a measurable nonnegative **real-valued** function and M>=1. Its tail premise is relative to the mass above M, at every real threshold at least M. The ratio conclusion uses any measure and may be an infinite extended inequality; the pure-moment result separately assumes a probability measure. It cannot be obtained by substituting p=0 into a formula containing 1/p. Application to a full infinite cluster must handle finiteness; natural cardinality alone assigns zero to an infinite set and is not a cluster-size substitute.

The determinacy theorem is for finite measures, with order zero included in the equal-moment premise, so their total masses agree. All absolute moments are assumed integrable for both measures. Nonnegative support transfers the summable absolute-moment series from one to the other. Equality of the resulting Laplace transforms is continued through the right half-plane and then to characteristic functions on its boundary by dominated convergence. The complex series lemma itself does not require nonnegative support. The separate moment-generating-function uniqueness theorem assumes equality as real functions and neighbourhood integrability for the first probability measure. No moment-convergence or tightness theorem, or standalone construction of the chi-square law, is proved in this file.

The hierarchy is finite through a specified P; the exported theorem has no extra assumption P>=3. The first two equivalents are explicit inputs, and differential identities are required only at orders 3 through P. The induction has coefficient (p+1) alpha and target exponent (2p-1) alpha, leaving the positive gap (p-2) alpha. The linear comparison allows arbitrary real limiting forcing c, including zero, but requires nonnegative S and local absolute continuity. The slowly varying extension requires a positive factor that is eventually C1 with vanishing logarithmic derivative; an arbitrary slowly varying function is not its hypothesis. No rate of convergence of the relative errors is assumed. No percolation derivative identity or error estimate is supplied by this analytic file.
