## Conventions and scope

FQCZ covers the integer-valued supremum norm, finite coordinate boxes and spheres, exact cardinalities, two forms of shell bounds, and radial grouping for extended nonnegative functions. The scalar natural-power inequalities are proved together by integrating the derivative on an interval of length two. Dimension zero is allowed in the exact counting and grouping statements; the positive-shell comparisons require positive dimension. The source represents the norm by its natural floor, with zero fiber exactly the origin.

6VNM gives the origin-zero power kernel for every real alpha, its nonnegativity and symmetry, and the exact positive-dimensional summability threshold. The comparison with a scalar power series retains both bounds, and the general equivalence between nonnegative summability and finite extended sum is stated separately in its proof. A divergent Lean real tsum has a default value; the article uses the extended sum when stating divergence and proves finiteness before using ordinary real tail sums.

The source tail is inclusive: norm at least r, for every real r>=1. The explicit constant is 2d 3^(d-1)(1+1/alpha). The article proves the one-step inequality, the finite telescoping estimate including N<m, and the infinite estimate. B2ZL is the older strictly omitted-row result with different constants, left unchanged. In dimension zero the origin-zero kernel vanishes for every exponent, so the positive-alpha equivalence does not extend there.

K38T uses the existing row identity R01V and degree criterion XJ74. The source assumes a pair intensity already compatible with an additive displacement kernel; since it is defined on unordered pairs, the compatibility implies inversion symmetry. No commutativity assumption is present. Countability supplies the probability statements and the simultaneous conclusion at all vertices. The off-diagonal kernel mass is the relevant divergent sum; finite diagonal intensity is irrelevant. Each kernel value is finite even when its total sum is infinite.

The two nonsummable source exports are the rooted and global threshold equalities. The article explains their infinite-degree mechanism, the zero-intensity endpoint and the power-kernel specialization.
