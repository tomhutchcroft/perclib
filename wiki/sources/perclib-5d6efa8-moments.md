## Hypotheses and the observation being measured

The moment windows observe intersections with full ambient clusters; paths may leave the windows. Roots can be outside them, and empty windows contribute zero. The pointwise expansions are deterministic on arbitrary vertex sets. The source probabilistic identities use a Bernoulli law on countable vertices and extended nonnegative integrals. The article proves the modest generalization to any configuration law, since neither the indicator expansion nor Tonelli uses independence. An infinite moment remains infinite, and the repeated marked vertex contributes to the ordinary second moment.

Cofinality requires every finite vertex set to lie in one window. This is stronger than the windows merely covering the vertex set. The source conclusion is a supremum, not convergence along an arbitrary enumeration. The article proves it by finite subsums and gives separate examples distinguishing these points; it does not interchange an arbitrary supremum and expectation.

Internal-domain independence, by contrast, uses the Bernoulli product law. General events must be measurable and determined by their internal coordinates. Ambient vertices can be arbitrary, and countability of each domain is enough for the internal-connection application. A measurable extension by closed exterior coordinates identifies a determined event with an event of the restricted product space; this is not conditioning on an infinite closed-boundary event. Finite mutual independence includes the empty family.

Disjoint windows alone do not make full-cluster observations independent. The article's four-vertex example makes both observed volumes equal to one plus the same Bernoulli variable. This illustrates the internal/full-cluster distinction without contradicting the source theorem. The moment witnesses cover a singleton, an empty window, an infinite second moment and a natural-number exhaustion; the domain witness uses two disjoint two-vertex domains. No new published-source erratum or compiled correspondence is asserted.
