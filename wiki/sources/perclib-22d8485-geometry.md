## Quadratic forms without infinite operators

The pointwise Gram identities allow arbitrary ambient vertices and repeated indices. The probabilistic identities use countable vertices and the bond product law, whereas the article also treats more general random graphs. All sums in the Gram identity are effectively finite. For Cauchy–Schwarz, positivity of a real quadratic polynomial gives the discriminant bound without dividing by a possibly zero diagonal form. Substituting the two endpoint rows into a finite restriction bounds each truncated cubic sum; every finite set of pairs fits in one square F times F. Taking suprema gives the extended triangle inequality without a summability hypothesis or an infinite operator. The diagonal bubble bound and regrouping identity have no countability premise. The two-vertex half-open model gives the matrix with diagonal one and off-diagonal one half.

These exports supply the product-law instances of 70KD, UZ7J and C2YT. They do not supply the article's open-triangle decay, invariant trace, or second diagram comparison.

## Intrinsic balls, spheres and thinning

The ball-membership recursion gives measurability on countable vertices, and the volume identity works under any measure with extended values. An empty sphere bounds only the root cluster, not disconnected vertices at infinite distance. Finite-radius balls may themselves be infinite. In the radius-volume estimate, r=floor(sqrt(n))+1 obeys the required two numerical bounds even at perfect squares. Arbitrary measures and infinite constants are allowed by the source; a finite useful tail estimate requires finite constants.

The thinning profile uses ratio one where the higher opening probability is zero, and the ratio of the opening probabilities elsewhere. For each fixed original configuration a short walk gives a finite distinct-edge certificate. Its conditional survival estimate bounds the measurable section of the connection event; no global measurable path-selection theorem is used. Countability enters the probability and summation steps, not the algebraic retention construction. The endpoint linear estimate chooses epsilon=b/(r+1), ensuring positivity of the lower intensity and entry into the assumed left neighbourhood. ## Comparing roots and partitioning a window

The root comparison first uses the two-point function and Harris, then the lower bound from a prescribed open walk. Repeated edges are counted only once in the walk product. The direct two-point comparison includes the infinite volume threshold; the explicit walk-tail export uses natural thresholds, including zero. Zero path factors still give valid extended inequalities. A positive support path is needed for the qualitative equivalences, and supplies no uniform comparison factor over an infinite component. The two-vertex half-open model is the witness.

For window sums, the fibres of the trace map have the trace's own cardinality. This gives the squared-size identity and both maximum bounds without a probability law or countable vertex type. The empty window gives zero. Measurability and expected identities require countable vertices in the source. Full clusters are observed through the window; they are not restricted to it. The expected squared-size sum is finite because it is at most the square of the window cardinality, even at infinite full-cluster susceptibility. Singleton and two-vertex empty configurations are the witnesses.
