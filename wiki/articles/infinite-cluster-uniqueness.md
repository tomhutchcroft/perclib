## Counting distinct components {#section-count}

<p>Let $N_\infty(\omega)$ be the number of distinct infinite connected components of the open graph, with value $\infty$ when there are infinitely many. Counting the roots whose clusters are infinite would give a different quantity: a single infinite cluster already contains infinitely many roots.</p>

<a id="tag-6GNC" data-assertion="theorem" data-title="Newman–Schulman trichotomy target"></a><p>Under the standing hypotheses, there is a deterministic $k\in\{0,1,\infty\}$ such that</p><div class="math-display">$$\mathbb P(N_\infty=k)=1.$$</div><p>This is the trichotomy statement: the number cannot be a finite integer larger than one, and it is almost surely constant.</p>

<p><em>Proof.</em> At $d=0$ the vertex set is a singleton and $N_\infty=0$. Suppose $d\geq1$, and work only with off-diagonal edge coordinates. For each finite $j\geq1$, the event $\{N_\infty\geq j\}$ is the countable union over vertex tuples $(x_1,\ldots,x_j)$ of the events that all their clusters are infinite and no two of them are connected. The <a href="/articles/infinite-cluster-zero-one/#section-zero-one">root infinite-cluster events</a> and connection events are measurable, so $N_\infty$ is measurable. It is invariant under translations.</p>
<p>Translation-invariant events have probability zero or one under this product law. Indeed, let $A$ be such an event and approximate it by a finite-coordinate event $B$ with $\mathbb P(A\mathbin\triangle B)&lt;\varepsilon$. Choose a translation $z$ so far away that the edge coordinates determining $B$ and its translate $zB$ are disjoint. Independence gives $\mathbb P(B\cap zB)=\mathbb P(B)^2$, while invariance of the law and of $A$ gives</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\left|\mathbb P(A)-\mathbb P(A)^2\right|\leq\left|\mathbb P(A\cap zA)-\mathbb P(B\cap zB)\right|+\left|\mathbb P(B)^2-\mathbb P(A)^2\right|\leq4\varepsilon.$$</div>
<p>Finite-coordinate approximation follows from the cylinder algebra as in the global zero-one proof. The disjoint translation exists because two fixed finite sets of lattice edges can overlap under only finitely many shifts. Let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Applying this conclusion to the nested events $\{N_\infty\geq j\}$ shows that $N_\infty$ has an almost surely constant value in $\{0,1,2,\ldots,\infty\}$.</p>
<p>Suppose this value were a finite integer $k\geq2$. Countability gives fixed vertices $x,y$ such that the event $A$ that $N_\infty=k$ and $x,y$ lie in distinct infinite clusters has positive probability. The support condition supplies a finite path from $x$ to $y$ whose edge probabilities are all positive: the allowed displacements and their negatives generate the lattice. Remove loops from this path, and let $F$ be its finite edge set.</p>
<p>Force every edge in $F$ to be open. On $A$, this merges at least the two infinite clusters containing $x,y$. Adding finitely many edges cannot create an infinite component solely from finite components, since it joins only finitely many of them. Thus the modified configuration has at most $k-1$ infinite clusters.</p>
<p>This modification has positive probability under the original law. To justify that assertion without assuming deletion tolerance, let $S$ be the set of configurations outside $F$ that admit at least one assignment on $F$ belonging to $A$. It is measurable, being a finite union of sections of $A$, and $\mathbb P_{F^c}(S)\geq\mathbb P(A)&gt;0$. The event that the outside configuration belongs to $S$ and all edges in $F$ are open has probability</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\mathbb P_{F^c}(S)\prod_{e\in F}q_e&gt;0.$$</div>
<p>Every configuration in this event has $N_\infty\leq k-1$, contradicting its almost sure value $k$. This excludes every finite value larger than one.</p>
<p>The proof uses spatial ergodicity for the number of infinite clusters, not invariance of that number under finite changes: adding a single edge may merge two infinite clusters. It allows probability-one bonds and infinite expected degree. It does not exclude infinitely many infinite clusters, which is the additional content of the uniqueness statement below.</p>

## Uniqueness {#section-uniqueness}

<a id="tag-T9KC" data-assertion="theorem" data-title="Long-range uniqueness target"></a><p>Under the same hypotheses,</p><div class="math-display">$$\mathbb P(N_\infty\leq1)=1.$$</div><p>No assumption $\sum_zp(z)&lt;\infty$ or $p(z)&lt;1$ is imposed. In particular, the statement allows infinite expected degree and probability-one bonds. It asserts at most one infinite cluster, not existence of an infinite cluster.</p>

<p><em>Idea of the proof.</em> By the <a href="/tag/6GNC/">trichotomy</a> it suffices to exclude $N_\infty=\infty$. The argument of Burton and Keane, extended to long-range models by Gandolfi, Keane and Newman (<em>Probability Theory and Related Fields</em> 92 (1992), 511–527, Theorem 0 on p. 513 and Theorem 1 on p. 516), runs as follows. If there are infinitely many infinite clusters, some finite box meets three of them with positive probability, and a change of the configuration on finitely many bonds makes the origin an encounter point: a vertex of an infinite cluster whose removal splits that cluster into at least three infinite clusters. By translation invariance the expected number of encounter points in a box $\Lambda_n$ is then at least a constant times $|\Lambda_n|$. On the other hand, removing $k$ encounter points of one cluster lying in $\Lambda_n$ leaves at least $k+2$ infinite pieces, and each piece, together with the bond that attached it to a removed point, contains its own open bond joining $\Lambda_n$ to its complement; the <a href="/tag/4K33/">finite-forest bound</a> is the combinatorial core of this count. When $\sum_zp(z)&lt;\infty$, the expected number of open bonds leaving $\Lambda_n$ is at most $\sum_zp(z)\,|\{x\in\Lambda_n:x+z\notin\Lambda_n\}|=o(|\Lambda_n|)$, a contradiction.</p>
<p>Two features of the statement fall outside this outline, and in both cases the open graph is almost surely connected, so that $N_\infty\leq1$. Suppose first that $p(u)=1$ for some $u\ne0$, and let $H$ be the subgroup generated by such $u$. Each coset $x+H$ is infinite and is connected by probability-one bonds. If $p(z)&gt;0$, the bonds $\{y,y+z\}$ with $y\in x+H$ are infinitely many distinct independent bonds, each open with probability $p(z)$. Almost surely one of them is open and joins $x+H$ to $x+z+H$. The support generates $\mathbb Z^d$, so almost surely all cosets lie in one cluster. Suppose next that $\sum_zp(z)=\infty$. Then the open graph is almost surely connected by the theorem of Grimmett, Keane and Marstrand (<em>Math. Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc.</em> 96 (1984), 151–166). Connectivity is an increasing event, and replacing $p$ by $\min(p,1/2)$ keeps the sum infinite and the support unchanged, so probability-one bonds do no harm there. The remaining case, $p(z)&lt;1$ for all $z\ne0$ and $\sum_zp(z)&lt;\infty$, is the one treated in the outline above.</p>

## Why the hypotheses matter {#section-scope}

<p>The support condition says that positive-probability bonds can connect the deterministic lattice: their allowed displacements generate it. Without this condition uniqueness can fail. For example, on $\mathbb Z$, let bonds of displacement $\pm2$ be open with probability one and all other non-loop bonds be closed. The even and odd integers are two distinct infinite clusters. The support generates $2\mathbb Z$, so this example does not satisfy the hypothesis.</p>

<p>Because bonds are unordered, the displacement condition already forces $p(z)=p(-z)$ for nonzero $z$. It does not constrain the diagonal coordinates. Those coordinates do not affect the graph, even if their probabilities vary from vertex to vertex. Translation invariance in this discussion is invariance of the open graph, not necessarily of the full configuration including loops.</p>

<p>At $d=0$ the graph has one vertex and no infinite component, so both conclusions hold. In positive dimension, probability-one nearest-neighbour bonds give a concrete connected support with one infinite cluster. Allowing such bonds is incompatible with a blanket deletion-tolerance requirement, which is why no such requirement should be silently added to the statement.</p>


## The finite-forest step in boundary counting {#section-forest-step}

<p>A common uniqueness argument represents selected encounter points by branch vertices of a finite forest and controls its leaves by a boundary set. Once such a forest has been constructed, the <a href="/tag/4K33/">finite-forest bound</a> gives $|B|\leq|L|$; for a nontrivial tree it gives the sharper $|B|+2\leq|L|$. The proof is the degree sum, with isolated vertices accounted for separately.</p>
<p>To turn this into a percolation uniqueness proof one must still construct the forest, relate encounter points to distinct branch vertices, and bound its leaves by a boundary quantity whose expected size is negligible compared with the volume. The finite combinatorial inequality alone proves none of these steps. In the present long-range setting, connections can jump over a nearest-neighbour boundary and the degree may be infinite. Thus this lemma does not complete the proof of <a href="/tag/T9KC/">T9KC</a> stated above.</p>
