## The real flow {#section-real-flow}

<p>Let $U_t=\sqrt\kappa B_t$ and $\partial_tg_t(z)=2/(g_t(z)-U_t)$. For a positive real starting point $x$, write $X_t=g_t(x)-U_t$, up to the first singularity. Thus</p>
<div class="math-display">$$dX_t=\frac2{X_t}\,dt-\sqrt\kappa\,dB_t,\qquad X_0=x.$$</div>

<a id="tag-RD3V" data-assertion="proposition" data-title="Real Loewner flows stay positive up to kappa four, with an explicit minimum law below four"></a>
<p>For $0\leq\kappa\leq4$, the solution exists and is strictly positive at every finite time, almost surely. For $0<\kappa<4$,</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\mathbb P\left(\inf_{t\geq0}X_t\leq a\right)
=\left(\frac ax\right)^{4/\kappa-1},\qquad 0<a<x.$$</div>
<p>In particular its infimum is strictly positive almost surely. At $\kappa=4$ the infimum is zero almost surely, although zero is never reached. At $\kappa=0$, $X_t=\sqrt{x^2+4t}$.</p>
<p><em>Proof.</em> Suppose first that $\kappa>0$. An increasing scale function is</p>
<div class="math-display">$$S(u)=
\begin{cases}-u^{-(4/\kappa-1)},&0<\kappa<4,\\
\log u,&\kappa=4.
\end{cases}$$</div>
<p>Its generator vanishes: $(\kappa/2)S''+2S'/u=0$. For $0<a<x<b$, let $T_a,T_b$ be the corresponding hitting times. On $[a,b]$ the diffusion coefficient is nonzero and the drift is bounded, so the exit time is finite almost surely. Bounded optional stopping gives</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\mathbb P_x(T_a<T_b)
=\frac{S(b)-S(x)}{S(b)-S(a)}.$$</div>
<p>Since $S(a)\to-\infty$ as $a\downarrow0$, the flow cannot reach zero before reaching any fixed upper level $b$. There is also no finite explosion to infinity. Indeed, stopping the identity</p>
<div class="math-display">$$d(X_t^2)=(4+\kappa)\,dt-2\sqrt\kappa X_t\,dB_t$$</div>
<p>at $T_a\wedge T_b\wedge T$ bounds the probability of reaching $b$ by that time by $[x^2+(4+\kappa)T]/b^2$. First let $a\downarrow0$, using the scale estimate, and then $b\to\infty$. Local existence away from zero now gives a global positive solution.</p>
<p>For fixed $a$, the events $\{T_a<T_b\}$ increase to $\{T_a<\infty\}$ as $b\to\infty$: a continuous path reaching $a$ at a finite time is bounded up to that time. The scale formula therefore gives $(a/x)^{4/\kappa-1}$ below four, and one at four. The distribution of the infimum follows by taking levels decreasing to $a$; their hitting probabilities have a continuous limit. Letting $a\downarrow0$ gives the asserted distinction between the two regimes. The zero-parameter case follows by solving the ordinary differential equation.</p>
<p>All assertions in this statement concern a fixed starting point. When boundary extensions of the flow are needed below, a countable set of starting points suffices. The difference of two solutions with $x>y>0$ satisfies</p>
<div class="math-display">$$g_t(x)-g_t(y)
=(x-y)\exp\left(-2\int_0^t\frac{ds}{(g_s(x)-U_s)(g_s(y)-U_s)}\right)>0.$$</div>
<p>Order preservation and positive rational starting points then exclude a singularity at any positive real point on a finite time interval. Reflection gives the negative half-line.</p>

## A bounded potential for distortion {#section-distortion}

<p>Fix $0<y<x$, and use the same Brownian motion in the two flows. Write $Y_t=g_t(y)-U_t$, $X_t=g_t(x)-U_t$, and set</p>
<div class="math-display">$$R_t=\frac{X_t-Y_t}{Y_t},\qquad
Q_t=\log g_t'(x)-\log(g_t(x)-g_t(y)).$$</div>
<p>The derivative is positive, and $Q_0=-\log(x-y)$.</p>

<a id="tag-JQ3M" data-assertion="theorem" data-title="A bounded potential controls boundary distortion for every kappa at most four"></a>
<p>For $0\leq\kappa\leq4$, the process $Q_t$ is increasing and</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\mathbb E\left[\sup_{t\geq0}(Q_t-Q_0)\right]
\leq H\left(\frac{x-y}{y}\right)<\frac{\pi^2}{6},\qquad
H(r)=\int_0^r\frac{\log(1+1/s)}{1+s}\,ds.$$</div>
<p>Consequently $\sup_{t\geq0}g_t'(x)/(g_t(x)-g_t(y))<\infty$ almost surely.</p>
<p><em>Proof.</em> Differentiating the flow and the difference of its two real solutions gives</p>
<div class="math-display">$$Q_t'=\frac{2R_t}{Y_t^2(1+R_t)^2}>0.$$</div>
<p>Itô's formula for their ratio gives</p>
<div class="math-display">$$dR_t=\frac{\sqrt\kappa R_t}{Y_t}\,dB_t
+\frac{R_t}{Y_t^2}\left(\kappa-2-\frac2{1+R_t}\right)dt.$$</div>
<p>The integral defining $H$ is finite at zero. With $v=s/(1+s)$,</p>
<div class="math-display">$$H(r)=\int_0^{r/(1+r)}\frac{-\log v}{1-v}\,dv,
\qquad H(\infty)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac1{n^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{6}.$$</div>
<p>In particular $0<H(r)<\pi^2/6$. Put $L(r)=\log(1+1/r)$. Differentiation gives $H'=L/(1+r)$ and $H''=-1/[r(1+r)^2]-L/(1+r)^2$. At $\kappa=4$, substituting these derivatives in the ratio generator gives drift $-Q_t'$ for $H(R_t)$. For general $\kappa$, the difference from this drift is</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\frac{\kappa-4}{Y_t^2}A(R_t),\qquad
A(r)=\frac{r}{2(1+r)^2}\bigl((r+2)\log(1+1/r)-1\bigr)>0.$$</div>
<p>The strict inequality follows from $\log(1+1/r)>1/(r+1)$, obtained by integrating $1/(1+u)>1/(1+1/r)$ on $0<u<1/r$.</p>
<p>It follows that $V_t=Q_t-Q_0+H(R_t)$ is a nonnegative local supermartingale for $\kappa\leq4$, and a local martingale at four. Localization on compact subsets of $\{0<Y<X<\infty\}$ is valid by RD3V and the positive difference formula. Fatou's lemma removes this stopping and gives $\mathbb E[V_t]\leq V_0=H(R_0)$. Since $Q_t-Q_0$ is nonnegative and increasing, monotone convergence proves the expectation bound. Its finiteness gives the almost-sure bound on $Q$, and exponentiation gives the final assertion.</p>
<p>Rohde–Schramm use the negative of this potential in the $\kappa=4$ part of Lemma 7.2, written using a dilogarithm. The integral form makes its boundedness immediate. The drift calculation above also proves the estimate for all smaller parameters; this extension is an argument here, not a further assertion attributed to the paper.</p>

## Staying away from a fixed boundary point {#section-boundary-distance}

<p>For the rest of the article assume the simple-trace theorem: $\gamma$ is continuous and injective, $\gamma(0)=0$, $\gamma(t)\in\mathbb H$ for $t>0$, and $g_t$ maps $\mathbb H\setminus\gamma[0,t]$ conformally onto $\mathbb H$. The inverse-map boundary limit at $U_t$ is $\gamma(t)$. These are named inputs from Rohde–Schramm Theorems 5.1 and 6.1.</p>

<a id="tag-YBCC" data-assertion="theorem" data-title="A fixed nonzero real point has positive distance from the entire SLE trace up to kappa four"></a>
<p>For $0\leq\kappa\leq4$, under this trace hypothesis and for each fixed $x\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$,</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\operatorname{dist}(x,\gamma[0,\infty))>0\qquad\text{almost surely}.$$</div>
<p><em>Proof.</em> Take $x>0$ and fix $0<y<x$. Reflect the slit across the real line and remove the ray $(-\infty,y]$. The resulting domain is</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\Omega_t=\mathbb C\setminus
\bigl(\gamma[0,t]\cup\overline{\gamma[0,t]}\cup(-\infty,y]\bigr),$$</div>
<p>where the bar on the second trace denotes complex conjugation. Schwarz reflection extends $g_t$ conformally to this domain. Indeed, it maps the upper and lower portions onto the two half-planes and maps the joining interval $(y,\infty)$ increasingly onto $(g_t(y),\infty)$. Hence its image is $\mathbb C\setminus(-\infty,g_t(y)]$.</p>
<p>The disk centered at $g_t(x)$ with radius $g_t(x)-g_t(y)$ lies in this image. Koebe's quarter theorem applied to $g_t^{-1}$ on that disk gives</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega_t)
\geq\frac{g_t(x)-g_t(y)}{4g_t'(x)}=\frac14e^{-Q_t}.$$</div>
<p>Since the trace is part of the removed set, its distance from $x$ is at least this lower bound. JQ3M makes the bound uniform in $t$ and strictly positive. Taking the infimum over time proves the result for $x>0$. Reflection invariance of Brownian motion gives the assertion for negative $x$.</p>
<p>This is stronger than merely saying that a fixed real point is never hit: it excludes approach to that point at arbitrarily large times. The statement keeps its fixed-point quantifier; no uncountable intersection of probability-one events is used.</p>

## Transience of a simple SLE trace {#section-transience}

<a id="tag-D67E" data-assertion="corollary" data-title="The simple SLE trace is transient for kappa at most four under the trace-generation hypothesis"></a>
<p>For $0\leq\kappa\leq4$, under the same simple-trace hypothesis,</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\lim_{t\to\infty}|\gamma(t)|=\infty\qquad\text{almost surely}.$$</div>
<p><em>Proof.</em> Cut the path at time one. The prime-end correspondence for a simple slit gives two finite boundary values $a<U_1<b$ of $g_1$ at the starting point $0$, one from each side of the slit. In particular, if points of $\mathbb H\setminus\gamma[0,1]$ approach zero, their images remain bounded and every subsequential limit belongs to $\{a,b\}$. This is the standard slit-boundary correspondence; it is the conformal-mapping input used at this step.</p>
<p>Conditionally on the path up to time one, the future trace mapped by $g_1$ and translated by $-U_1$ has the law of a fresh ordinary SLE trace. This follows from the Loewner composition rule and the independence of $B_{1+t}-B_1$. The two numbers $a-U_1$ and $b-U_1$ are then fixed nonzero real points. Applying YBCC to each of them shows that the mapped future stays a positive distance from both. The slit-boundary correspondence therefore precludes a sequence of future trace points tending to zero. Thus</p>
<div class="math-display">$$D:=\inf_{t\geq1}|\gamma(t)|>0\qquad\text{almost surely}.$$</div>
<p>For $T>0$, Brownian scaling and the Loewner equation give</p>
<div class="math-display">$$D_T:=\inf_{t\geq T}|\gamma(t)|\ \overset{\mathrm{law}}=\ \sqrt T\,D.$$</div>
<p>For any fixed radius $R$, it follows that $\mathbb P(D_T\leq R)=\mathbb P(D\leq R/\sqrt T)\to0$. The events $\{D_T\leq R\}$ decrease as $T$ increases, so with probability one the tail eventually has distance greater than $R$ from zero. Taking integer radii proves transience.</p>
<p>This proves the simple-curve case of Rohde–Schramm Theorem 7.1 from the stated trace and conformal-mapping inputs. It does not prove the trace-construction theorem, the simple-trace theorem, or transience for $\kappa>4$.</p>


## Continuity and simplicity are separate inputs {#section-generation-update}

<p>For the range from zero to four, <a href="/tag/MN2A/">MN2A</a> now supplies the continuous generating trace and full inverse-map limits. The simple-trace hypothesis used in the geometric arguments above is still a separate assertion, Rohde–Schramm Theorem 6.1. The trace-generation proof does not establish injectivity.</p>


## From real-flow avoidance to simplicity {#section-simplicity}

<p>We now supply that remaining hypothesis. In this section simplicity is not assumed: we use the continuous generating trace from <a href="/tag/MN2A/">MN2A</a> and the real-flow theorem RD3V. Put $K_t=\overline{\mathbb H\setminus D_t}$, with closure in $\mathbb C$. In particular $K_0=\varnothing$; the starting point of the trace will be included separately when $t=0$.</p>

<a id="tag-W8MU" data-assertion="proposition" data-title="Up to kappa four the SLE trace and finite hulls avoid every nonzero real point simultaneously"></a>
<p>For $0\leq\kappa\leq4$, almost surely</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\gamma[0,\infty)\cap\mathbb R\subseteq\{0\},\qquad
K_t\cap\mathbb R\subseteq\{0\}\quad\text{for every }t\geq0.$$</div>
<p>Moreover, for every $x\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$ and finite $T$, the forward maps $g_s$, $0\leq s\leq T$, extend holomorphically to a common neighborhood of $x$ and satisfy $g_s(x)\ne U_s$ throughout that interval.</p>

<p><em>Proof.</em> Intersect the probability-one events of RD3V over positive rational starting points. On this event fix $x&gt;0$, a rational $q\in(0,x)$ and $T&lt;\infty$. Until the flow from $x$ ceases to exist, order preservation gives</p>
<div class="math-display">$$g_s(x)-U_s&gt;g_s(q)-U_s\geq\delta,
\qquad \delta=\min_{0\leq s\leq T}(g_s(q)-U_s)&gt;0.$$</div>
<p>The drift of $g_s(x)$ is consequently bounded by $2/\delta$. It cannot escape to infinity on this interval or reach the pole, so the solution continues through time $T$. This argument is pathwise for every $x&gt;0$ and every finite $T$. Applying RD3V to the reflected Brownian driving function and intersecting the two events gives the corresponding assertion on the negative half-line.</p>

<p>Fix one of these nonzero real points. Its flow stays a positive distance from the moving pole on $[0,T]$. Continuous and holomorphic dependence of the complex Loewner equation on the initial value therefore gives a neighborhood $N$ of $x$ on which the solutions exist throughout $[0,T]$ and stay away from the pole. For $z\in N\cap\mathbb H$,</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\operatorname{Im}g_s(z)
=\operatorname{Im}z\,\exp\left(-2\int_0^s\frac{du}{|g_u(z)-U_u|^2}\right)&gt;0.$$</div>
<p>Thus $N\cap\mathbb H\subset D_T$. A smaller neighborhood of $x$ misses $K_T$, proving the hull assertion. If $\gamma(s)=x$ at some $s\leq T$, then $f_s(U_s+iy)\to x$ as $y\downarrow0$. Continuity of the extended $g_s$ would give $g_s(x)=U_s$, a contradiction. This proves the trace assertion. No intersection over the uncountable set of real starting points or times was taken.</p>

<a id="tag-CKFH" data-assertion="theorem" data-title="Ordinary SLE is a simple interior trace for kappa at most four"></a>
<p>For $0\leq\kappa\leq4$, almost surely $\gamma$ is injective, $\gamma(t)\in\mathbb H$ for every $t&gt;0$, and</p>
<div class="math-display">$$D_t=\mathbb H\setminus\gamma[0,t]\quad(t\geq0),\qquad
K_t=\gamma[0,t]\quad(t&gt;0).$$</div>
<p>More precisely, on the same event,</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\gamma[0,a]\cap\gamma[b,\infty)=\varnothing
\qquad\text{whenever }0\leq a&lt;b.$$</div>

<p><em>Proof.</em> For each nonnegative rational $s$, restart the Loewner chain at time $s$ in coordinates translated by $-U_s$. Its driving function is $U_{s+u}-U_s$, so the restarted chain has the ordinary SLE law. On one probability-one event, MN2A and W8MU apply to the original chain and to every such restart. Let $\beta_s$ be the restarted trace. Then</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\beta_s[0,\infty)\subset\mathbb H\cup\{0\}.$$</div>
<p>The inverse-flow composition identity and the full boundary continuity in MN2A give</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\gamma(s+u)=f_s\bigl(U_s+\beta_s(u)\bigr)
\qquad(u\geq0).$$</div>
<p>To see the boundary step, first write the composition for inverse maps at positive height above the restarted driving point, and then let the height decrease to zero. Both limits exist, and $f_s$ is continuous at the limiting argument. This avoids applying $g_s$ to a future trace point before knowing that the point lies in $D_s$.</p>

<p>If $\beta_s(u)\in\mathbb H$, its image lies in $D_s$. If $\beta_s(u)=0$, its image is $f_s(U_s)=\gamma(s)$. Hence, simultaneously for every rational $s\geq0$,</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\gamma[s,\infty)\cap(\mathbb R\cup K_s)=\{\gamma(s)\}.$$</div>
<p>This does not yet exclude a later return to $\gamma(s)$. Strict growth of capacity supplies the additional information.</p>

<p>Fix arbitrary $0\leq a&lt;b$. The domains $D_a$ and $D_b$ are different: otherwise their hydrodynamically normalized maps would be equal, contradicting their respective coefficients $2a/z$ and $2b/z$ at infinity. Some point of $\gamma[a,b]$ must therefore lie in $D_a$. Indeed, if the whole added curve avoided $D_a$, that connected unbounded domain would still lie in the unbounded component of $\mathbb H\setminus\gamma[0,b]$, giving $D_a\subseteq D_b$ and hence equality.</p>

<p>Since $\gamma(a)\notin D_a$, continuity and openness of $D_a$ give a nonempty open interval inside $(a,b)$ on which the trace lies in $D_a$. Choose a rational $s$ there. Then $\gamma(s)\notin\mathbb R\cup K_a$, whereas</p>
<div class="math-display">$$\gamma[0,a]\subseteq\mathbb R\cup K_a
\subseteq\mathbb R\cup K_s.$$</div>
<p>The restart conclusion says that the later trace can meet the last set only at $\gamma(s)$, which is outside $\gamma[0,a]$. Thus $\gamma[0,a]\cap\gamma[b,\infty)=\varnothing$. This reasoning is pathwise for every pair $a&lt;b$ on the single event already chosen. It proves injectivity, including exclusion of a return to $\gamma(0)=0$. W8MU then puts every positive-time trace point in $\mathbb H$.</p>

<p>For a fixed positive time, $\gamma[0,t]$ is now a Jordan arc with one endpoint on the real line and every other point in $\mathbb H$. The planar Jordan-arc separation theorem says that such a slit does not disconnect $\mathbb H$; this elementary topological fact is the remaining input here. Therefore the unbounded component in MN2A is all of $\mathbb H\setminus\gamma[0,t]$. Taking the closure of its complement in $\mathbb H$ gives $K_t=\gamma[0,t]$. At $t=0$, the domain equality still holds, while our definition gives $K_0=\varnothing$.</p>

<p>These arguments supply Rohde–Schramm Lemma 6.2 and Theorem 6.1 from the earlier written real-flow and trace-construction proofs. Consequently the simple-trace hypotheses of YBCC and D67E are now satisfied throughout $0\leq\kappa\leq4$. Their conclusions give positive distance from each fixed nonzero real point and transience for ordinary SLE in this range, using the conformal-mapping inputs already stated there. The fixed-point distance assertion is not replaced by an uncountable simultaneous positive-distance claim.</p>
